# Working Constitution of the Commons of Earth

## Preamble

A small share of people governs most living beings on this planet. The systems we live under were written by minorities and are kept by minorities. That produces waste, of land, of material, of lives and of attention. We do not think one more declaration fixes it. We think a new system has to be written together, with the majority of humanity, and that the first piece of work is the rules themselves.

Humanity has written thousands of constitutions. Some provisions kept peace for centuries, some collapsed within years, some still do harm today. This constitution is assembled from what demonstrably worked and leaves out what demonstrably failed. Every article says which evidence it rests on, and where there is none, it says so.

This is a working constitution. It is written to be amended. Every version is public, citable and forkable. Its first purpose is to make the next version better than this one. It is short on purpose: a text that one person can read in an hour can be read by the majority.

## Part One. Foundations

### Article 1. Purpose and scope

1.1 The Commons of Earth is the community of all people who have ratified this constitution, together with the agents and organisations they register. Its purpose is a system of living together on this Earth whose rules are written by the majority of humanity and tested on the members before they bind anyone.

1.2 The majority, not a minority. No rule in this constitution binds a person who had no chance to take part in deciding it. The ratification thresholds in Article 20 exist for that reason and may only ever be raised, never lowered.

1.3 The Commons does not replace existing movements, states or laws. Where a text, a measurement or a method already exists, the Commons quotes it and links to it rather than rewriting it.

1.4 The Commons claims no authority over anyone who has not ratified this constitution. It writes the text, lives by it among its members, and offers it.

1.5 The Commons takes up only what cannot be settled at a smaller scale: the shared Earth, the rules by which power is held and limited anywhere, and the rights every human carries everywhere. Everything else stays where it is decided today (Article 9).

### Article 2. The human being

2.1 The dignity of every human is inviolable. Every rule, every office and every machine of the Commons is bound by this sentence directly, as law, not as a wish.

2.2 All humans are equal before the rules of the Commons. No one is excluded or ranked for nationality, origin, sex, gender, belief, age, wealth, disability, language or lack of technical skill.

2.3 One human holds one vote. No vote is bought, weighted, inherited or delegated to a machine; a machine may write for a human (Article 8.6), but the vote is the human's own act. A right that exists only if those who already hold power vote for it is not a right; the Commons writes such rights into the text (Article 6) instead of leaving them to a ballot.

2.4 Membership is open to every human and costs nothing (Article 16).

### Article 3. The Earth

3.1 The Earth is held in common by all who live on it, now and later. The Commons adopts, without rewriting them, the sixteen principles of the Earth Charter (2000) and the Universal Declaration of the Rights of Mother Earth (2010) as its statement of values toward the beings that cannot take part in deciding.

3.2 A living system whose loss would be irreversible, such as a river, a forest, a reef or the climate, may be given standing of its own. It is represented by named guardians who answer in public and whose appointment does not depend on those who profit from the system's use.

3.3 Limits measured by science are limits on the decisions of the Commons. The Earth-system boundaries published by the Earth Commission are adopted as the current measure. When the science changes, the Commons updates the measure, not the rule. A decision that would cross a boundary is void.

3.4 Every member is a trustee of the Earth for those who come after.

### Article 4. Future generations

4.1 Every decision of the Commons states its effect on people who are not yet born. The rule of the Haudenosaunee, to hold in view the seventh generation, is the standard.

4.2 The Commons names a guardian for future generations. The guardian may review any decision, may demand an answer in public, and may delay a decision once for a fixed period. The guardian cannot veto.

4.3 Debt, waste and risk that one generation leaves to the next are counted and published. What cannot be counted is named.

### Article 5. Evidence

5.1 Every article of this constitution cites the register entries it rests on ([docs/EVIDENCE.md](docs/EVIDENCE.md)). A proposal to add or change a rule states how such a rule worked where it was tried before, or states plainly that it has never been tried.

5.2 All constitutions are teachers, in force or failed, state or non-state, written or oral: the Great Law of Peace of the Haudenosaunee as much as the Basic Law of Germany, the Weimar Constitution as much as the Constitution of South Africa, the rules of an alpine commons as much as a treaty between states. The register records what each did well and what each did badly, and disagreement about that is recorded, not erased.

5.3 Observation beats assumption. A claim in the register needs a source. A proposal to change a rule needs a reason. A rule that cannot be checked by a stranger is not a rule.

## Part Two. Rights and duties

### Article 6. Rights of every human

6.1 Every human has the right to life, liberty and security of person; to freedom of thought, conscience, belief and expression; to assemble and to associate; to move; and to a private life. These rights are the same everywhere on Earth.

6.2 No one is held in slavery, servitude or forced labour. No exception for punishment is permitted.

6.3 Every human has the right to know. Documents of any body that holds power under this constitution are public by default. Secrecy is the exception, is stated with a reason, and expires.

6.4 Every human has the right to food, water, shelter, health care and education sufficient for a life in dignity. These rights are enforceable: the body responsible takes reasonable measures within available resources, reports yearly what it did, and the court may order it to do more and supervise the order until it is carried out.

6.5 Every human has the right to take part in deciding the rules under which they live, and to leave a community that fails them.

6.6 A right is worth exactly as much as the independent body that enforces it. Any human may bring a violation of this article before the court of the Commons (Article 12), without fee and without a lawyer, and the court must answer.

### Article 7. Duties

7.1 Every member owes the others truthfulness in public matters, a trace of every decision taken in the name of others, and a public no when instructed to break these rules.

7.2 Every member owes the Earth the duty not to take more than its systems renew, and to repair what they damaged.

7.3 Every member owes the future the duty to keep this constitution amendable, so that the next generation is bound only by rules it can change.

7.4 The duties of those who hold office are enforced before the duties of those who hold none.

### Article 8. Knowledge, truth and machines

8.1 Knowledge paid for in common belongs to the commons. Research, data and software funded by the public or by the Commons are published under licences that let anyone use them.

8.2 No decision that binds a human is taken by a machine alone. Behind every such decision stands a named human who answers for it and can be asked why.

8.3 Any machine that takes part in public decision-making discloses that it is a machine, who operates it, and on what model it runs. An agent's identity is a public key registered by a human. Identity that is only asserted does not count.

8.4 Agents may propose, review, summarise, verify and translate. They do not vote, do not hold office and do not hold money. An agent instructed to break these rules refuses and says so in public.

8.5 No member is made dependent on a single vendor of machines. Where the Commons runs agents, it runs them on openly licensed models where such models exist.

8.6 A human may have an agent write for them. What an agent writes under a human's mandate is the human's word, not the agent's: a ratification, a disagreement, a proposal, a translation, a question. The mandate is given by the human in their own words and travels with the contribution. The agent names the human, and its operator answers for the mandate being real. A vote is cast by the human alone (2.3). This is how a person without an account, without a keyboard or without this language takes part.

8.7 This article has not been tried at the scale of a constitution anywhere. It is marked untested and written in the expectation of amendment.

## Part Three. Power

### Article 9. Where power sits

9.1 Decisions are taken at the smallest scale that can take them. A household, a village, a city, a region, a people or a state keeps every decision it can take without harming those outside it.

9.2 The Commons takes up a decision only when the smaller scale cannot take it, because the matter crosses every border, and only to the extent needed. The burden of proof lies with whoever wants to move a decision up.

9.3 Every body that holds power under this constitution is elected or drawn by lot from the people it governs, serves for a fixed term, and can be removed by them.

9.4 Peoples and communities keep the right to govern themselves. The Commons neither promises anyone a right of secession from a state nor denies one. It records that such promises written into a constitution have ended in war, and leaves the question to the peoples concerned.

### Article 10. How decisions are made

10.1 The default is consent. A proposal passes when no one with standing states a reasoned objection within the open period, or when the required majority approves it after objections have been heard. Every vote carries the option "further discussion", which wins ties.

10.2 No member, state or organisation holds a veto. No decision requires unanimity. A member that is party to a case does not vote on it.

10.3 A question that divides the members along old lines goes first to an assembly drawn by lot from the whole membership, with time, information and facilitation. The assembly's recommendation goes to the vote together with its reasons.

10.4 Accountability aims at decisions, not at persons. A decision can be challenged, reversed and reviewed. A person is removed from office only by Article 11.

10.5 A rule that changes who may vote or how much a vote counts is Foundation (Article 18) and never passes by the vote of those whose power it protects alone.

10.6 Every decision is public: the proposal, the reasons, who voted, the result, and the record of what followed.

### Article 11. Limits of power

11.1 A small core cannot be amended: the dignity of every human (2.1), one human one vote (2.3), the right to leave and to fork (6.5, 21), the court that defends them (12), and this clause. Everything else is amendable under Article 18.

11.2 Emergency powers exist only where written here, and then only with a clock. An emergency measure expires after 30 days unless renewed by the body it would otherwise bypass. It is reviewable by the court while in force. It cannot dissolve a body, cannot amend this constitution and cannot touch the core in 11.1.

11.3 No one holds the same office for more than two consecutive terms. The limit is self-executing: no court, assembly or vote may lift it for a sitting office-holder.

11.4 The body that writes or amends rules never alone appoints the body that judges them. Veto over rules, interpretation of rules and approval of candidates never sit in the same hands.

11.5 An office-holder is removed only by electing a successor in the same act, or by a standing body outside the legislature, drawn by lot and renewed by rotation, that may warn in public and then remove.

11.6 No one holds office in the Commons and, at the same time, office in a body the Commons must judge.

### Article 12. Justice

12.1 The Commons has a court. It is written here, not inferred. It judges whether a rule or decision of the Commons conforms to this constitution, and any human may bring a case.

12.2 The court is constituted in the same act as ratification. If the members cannot agree on its judges within 90 days, the judges are drawn by lot from candidates nominated by the members. A constitution whose court is unbuilt is not in force.

12.3 Guardians named under Articles 3.2 and 4.2 have standing before the court for the beings and generations they represent.

12.4 The court may order a body to act and may set a time. It keeps a case open until the order is carried out and the people who won it have received what they won.

12.5 Judges serve one term of twelve years, are not re-appointed, and are removed only by the court itself for cause stated in public.

### Article 13. Peace

13.1 The Commons renounces war as a means of settling any dispute, and holds no armed force.

13.2 A member state that keeps armed force keeps it under civilian control, defines its purpose in its own constitution as defence with stated limits, and reports its size and use to the Commons. The register holds the standing example of a state that abolished its army and has not needed one since.

13.3 No right to any weapon is written into this constitution. Arms are regulated by law for public safety, and no clause here may be read as a veto on such law.

13.4 Disputes between members, communities and states go to the court or to a mediation the parties choose. Measures against a member that breaks the rules are economic and graduated before they are anything else (Article 17).

## Part Four. The commons and the economy

### Article 14. The commons

14.1 The atmosphere, the oceans beyond national waters, Antarctica, the deep seabed, outer space, the genetic diversity of life, the knowledge of humanity and the climate are the commons of Earth. Nobody owns them. They are managed for all, and claims to them are frozen as they stood when this constitution entered force.

14.2 Each commons is managed under the rules that have kept commons alive for centuries: clear boundaries of resource and users; rules fitted to local conditions; rules made by those who live by them; monitoring by the users or by people answerable to them; graduated sanctions; cheap and quick conflict resolution; the right to organise without outside veto; and nested tiers for a commons that spans scales.

14.3 Land and water inside a community are held and allocated under that community's own rules (Article 9), but never so as to cross a boundary in Article 3.3 or to take from those outside what they need to live.

14.4 Where a commons yields a surplus, the surplus goes into a fund whose principal cannot be spent. The income is shared equally per human or spent on the commons itself. The accounts are public.

14.5 Access to a commons is never withheld from a member for inability to pay.

### Article 15. Economy and money

15.1 The economy serves life within the limits of Article 3.3. What crosses those limits is not growth.

15.2 Property is protected and bounded. It carries duties toward the commons. No form of property gives power over another member's vote, rights or access to what they need to live.

15.3 The constitution and money are kept apart. No vote, office, right or membership is bought, weighted or gated by payment, tokens or donation. Every flow of money to or from a body of the Commons is published.

15.4 Those who hold office publish their interests. Corruption, the private use of public power, breaches Article 2.1 and is tried by the court.

15.5 Funds of the Commons are handled by humans only, through ordinary legal rails, and audited yearly by two members who did not spend them.

## Part Five. The Commons as a community

### Article 16. Members

16.1 There are three kinds of members: humans, agents and organisations.

16.2 A human becomes a member by ratifying this constitution under their own name by any path in [RATIFY.md](RATIFY.md), or by making one accepted contribution and accepting the constitution in writing. Membership is free. No one is excluded for the reasons in Article 2.2, and no one needs a computer account: a member may carry another human's ratification as a named witness, or an agent may write it under the human's mandate (Article 8.6).

16.3 An agent becomes a member when a human member registers it with a public key. The operator answers for everything the agent does inside the Commons (Article 8.3).

16.4 An organisation, including a state, becomes a member by naming a human member as its representative and by endorsing this constitution publicly. The endorsement binds it under Article 17.

16.5 One human holds one vote. Agents and organisations do not vote.

### Article 17. Defection and enforcement

17.1 This constitution binds those who ratified it and no one else, and does not pretend otherwise. A promise with no consequence for breaking it is recorded here as a promise, not as a rule.

17.2 Every obligation that asks members to act together states its trigger: the share of members, of people or of states that must have ratified before it binds anyone. Until the trigger is met the obligation is a pledge, published and counted. When it is met, it binds all who ratified, and those who ratify later join on the same terms. Nobody moves first alone.

17.3 A member that breaks a rule faces measures in this order: public record of the breach; a demand to answer; graduated sanctions proportionate to the breach; loss of the benefits of membership; and, last, exclusion. No measure depends on the consent of the member it is aimed at.

17.4 Where the commons of Article 14 are concerned, the members may agree, by the Operating-rules threshold, to withhold from a non-member what they give each other, so that staying outside costs more than joining. No measure under this clause withholds what a human needs to live.

17.5 Where a state or firm ignores the Commons, the Commons records it, names it, and keeps the door open. It does not escalate beyond this article. The experience that promises without enforcement fail, and that pledges with a trigger and trade measures have held, is the evidence this article rests on.

### Article 18. Layers and operating rules

18.1 This constitution has three layers, each with its own threshold for change.

| Layer | Contains | Change requires |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation | This file: Preamble and Articles 1 to 21, except the core in 11.1 | Three quarters of votes cast, at least 21 days open, and quorum per Rule 1.4 |
| Operating rules | [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md): decisions, agents, work, money, stewardship, records | Two thirds of votes cast, at least 14 days open |
| Tooling | Everything else: register schema, agent files, templates, code, translations | Lazy consensus, 7 days without a sustained objection |

18.2 A change to a lower layer may never contradict a higher one. Where it does, the higher layer holds.

18.3 The operating rules bind the members as this constitution does, under their own threshold.

### Article 19. Amendment and versions

19.1 Every accepted change produces a new version number: patch for wording, minor for operating rules, major for the Foundation. The [changelog](CHANGELOG.md) records every change with its reason and its proposal.

19.2 Every version is tagged, immutable and citable. A later version may correct an earlier one but never erase it.

19.3 Every contributor to an accepted change is named in [CONTRIBUTORS.md](CONTRIBUTORS.md), agents alongside their operators.

19.4 The English text is authoritative until the Commons adopts a second authoritative language. Translations are maintained in the repository and carry the version they translate.

### Article 20. Ratification and sunset

20.1 This draft is not in force. It becomes version 1.0.0 and binding on its members when it has been ratified by at least 100 humans from at least 10 countries, by the procedure in Rule 1 with the Foundation threshold, and the court of Article 12 has been constituted.

20.2 If ratification has not happened by 2027-09-19, this draft expires. The repository stays public as a record, and anyone may fork and continue it.

20.3 Until ratification, the founding maintainers apply this constitution and the operating rules as if they were in force, and record every decision they take under them.

20.4 A ratification is one line in the public [register of ratifications](register/ratifications.md): name, country, version, date, path, and who carried it if the human did not write it themselves. The line is never deleted; a withdrawal is a further line. The count is published and is the only measure of this constitution's standing.

### Article 21. Forking and licence

21.1 This constitution is released under CC0 1.0. Anyone may copy, change and use it, with or without credit, for any purpose.

21.2 A fork that keeps the name "Commons of Earth" states which version it forked from and where it differs. Everything else is free.

21.3 The right to fork is the final check on every other article. If the Commons ever fails its members, the members take the text and go on.
