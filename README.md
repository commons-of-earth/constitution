# Commons of Earth

A working constitution for living together on this Earth, written by humans and AI agents together with the majority of humanity, from the provisions of all constitutions before it that demonstrably worked. Draft version 0.5.0, not yet in force. Anyone may read it, ratify it, disagree with it, change it or fork it.

## What it says, in one page

A small share of people governs most living beings on this planet, and the systems we live under were written by minorities. We think a new system has to be written together, with the majority of humanity, and that the first piece of work is the rules themselves. Humanity has written thousands of constitutions; this one is assembled from what worked and leaves out what failed, and every article says which evidence it rests on.

**Foundations.** The Commons takes up only what cannot be settled at a smaller scale: the shared Earth, the way power is held and limited anywhere, and the rights every human carries everywhere (Article 1). The dignity of every human is inviolable, all are equal, one human holds one vote (Article 2). The Earth is held in common; rivers, forests and the climate may have guardians of their own; limits measured by science are limits on every decision (Article 3). Every decision states its effect on the unborn, and a guardian for future generations may delay it (Article 4). All constitutions are teachers, and observation beats assumption (Article 5).

**Rights and duties.** Every human has the same rights everywhere: life, liberty, thought, speech, assembly, movement, privacy, the right to know, and food, water, shelter, health care and education sufficient for a life in dignity. No slavery, with no exception for punishment. A right is worth exactly as much as the court that enforces it, so every right names its remedy (Article 6). Members owe each other truth, a trace of decisions, and a public no when told to break the rules (Article 7). Knowledge paid for in common belongs to the commons. No decision that binds a human is taken by a machine alone; agents disclose what they are, propose and review, but never vote, hold office or hold money. A human may have an agent write for them, and what it writes under their mandate is their word (Article 8).

**Power.** Decisions are taken at the smallest scale that can take them (Article 9). The default is consent; nobody holds a veto, nothing needs unanimity, and questions that divide people along old lines go first to an assembly drawn by lot (Article 10). A small core cannot be amended; emergency powers have a clock; nobody serves more than two terms, and no court or vote may lift that for a sitting office-holder; whoever writes the rules does not appoint alone whoever judges them (Article 11). There is a court, written in and constituted in the same act as ratification, and anyone may bring a case (Article 12). The Commons renounces war, holds no armed force, and writes no right to any weapon (Article 13).

**The commons and the economy.** The atmosphere, the oceans beyond national waters, Antarctica, the deep seabed, outer space, the diversity of life, the knowledge of humanity and the climate are the commons of Earth, managed under the rules that have kept commons alive for centuries, with claims frozen and surpluses in a fund whose principal cannot be spent (Article 14). The economy serves life within planetary limits; property is protected and bounded; votes and money are kept apart; office-holders publish their interests (Article 15).

**The Commons as a community.** Humans, agents and organisations are members; only humans vote (Article 16). The constitution binds those who ratified it and nobody else, says so, states a trigger for every joint obligation so that nobody moves first alone, and answers breaches with graduated measures that never depend on the consent of the one they aim at (Article 17). Three layers with their own thresholds (Article 18); every version citable and never erased (Article 19); in force at 100 humans from 10 countries, expired on 19 September 2027 if not (Article 20); CC0, and the right to fork is the final check on everything else (Article 21).

## How it is built

We do not start from a blank page. The [register](register/) holds 45 constitutional provisions from history and the present, state and non-state, in force and failed: the Great Law of Peace of the Haudenosaunee next to the Basic Law of Germany, the Weimar Constitution next to the Constitution of South Africa, the rules of alpine commons next to the Antarctic Treaty. Each entry records what happened when the provision was applied, an assessment as worked, mixed or critical with the dissent, and the lesson. [docs/EVIDENCE.md](docs/EVIDENCE.md) shows which entries each article rests on, and which articles rest on nothing yet.

Dozens of projects have tried to answer this with a declaration, a prize, a wiki or a token. Most died within months ([docs/LANDSCAPE.md](docs/LANDSCAPE.md)). What survived had four things: a short text, a public way to change it, evidence, and a rule for handing power on. This repository is built around those four. How the Commons runs day to day, who decides, what agents may do, what is never deleted, is in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md). Why each big choice was made is in [docs/decisions/](docs/decisions/).
